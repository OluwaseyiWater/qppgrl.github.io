QPPG: Quantum-Preconditioned Policy Gradient for Link Adaptation in Rayleigh Fading Channels

Oluwaseyi Giwa1, Muhammad Ahmed Mohsin2, Folarin Jubril Adesola3, Muhammad Ali Jamshed4
1African Institute for Mathematical Sciences, 2Stanford University, 3Olabisi Onabanjo University, 4University of Glasgow
PDF arXiv Code

The QPPG Framework. The Quantum-Geometric Preconditioner transforms $g$ into the natural gradient $x$ by solving a regularized linear system using CG Solver and FVP.

Abstract

Reliable link adaptation is critical for 6G wireless communications in dynamic fading environments. However, classical reinforcement learning (RL) often suffers from unstable convergence due to the Euclidean geometry of the standard gradient updates, which ignores the curvature of the policy parameter space. We propose the quantum-preconditioned policy gradient (QPPG), a geometric optimization framework that approximates the quantum natural gradient (QNG) for classical link adaptation. We stress that the prefix quantum refers solely to the Fubini-Study metric and quantum-tomography-inspired regularization from which the preconditioner is derived, not to quantum hardware or exponential speedups. Evaluations across five network scenarios demonstrate that QPPG bridges the gap between quantum geometry and classical engineering. It achieves a 28.6% gain in throughput and a 43.8% decrease in transmit power compared to classical baselines, while exhibiting superior robustness to channel estimation errors and noise uncertainty.

Distinctions from State-of-the-Art

QPPG vs. TRPO and Damped NPG


Algorithmically, QPPG operates as a Tikhonov-regularized (damped) natural policy gradient utilizing FVP and CG. We state directly that QPPG is fundamentally a quantum-motivated version of damped NPG rather than a new algorithmic update rule. However, unlike TRPO's hard KL constraints, which can cause step-size stagnation during SNR spikes, QPPG's damping $\xi$ is motivated by quantum-state tomography. This provides an information-geometric rationale for regularizing against noisy observations, moving beyond heuristic damping selection to stabilize learning in fading environments.


QPPG vs. QAC
QAC uses Parameterized Quantum Circuits (model-level innovation) but often updates via Euclidean gradients, risking barren plateaus. QPPG maintains a classical network but modifies the optimization landscape via preconditioning to counteract high-variance fading.

Complexity and Scalability

The computational cost of QPPG is dominated by the CG solver. While standard policy gradients scale linearly with the number of parameters \(d\) (i.e., \(\mathcal{O}(d)\)), QPPG requires \(K_{\rm{CG}}\) forward-backward passes for FVP, yielding a per-step complexity of \(\mathcal{O}(K_{\rm{CG}} \cdot d)\). In well-conditioned scenarios (e.g., s1, s2), where the FIM is full-rank, and QAC achieves comparable final reward, the additional \(K_{\rm{CG}} \cdot d\) overhead is less justifiable. However, in near-singular landscapes (s4, s5), QPPG reaches $90%$ of its maximum reward in approximately $400$ episodes, compared to $> 800$ episodes for classical baselines. This roughly $2\times$ improvement in sample efficiency directly offsets the higher per-iteration cost, rendering the overhead practically worthwhile in high-uncertainty environments.
